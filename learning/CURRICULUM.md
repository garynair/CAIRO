# Chief AI Risk Officer Curriculum

**Role level:** 70 (executive AI strategy — AI Governance family)
**Family:** AI Governance
**Status:** planned — modules and projects below are the planned scope keyed to the internal curriculum plan. All 12 modules and 5 projects are already scaffolded under [`lessons/`](lessons/) and [`projects/`](projects/). Lecture notes, exercises, quizzes, and reference solutions are populated for the foundational cycle; subsequent research cycles will refresh regulatory citations and add depth as the emerging themes catalogued in the internal curriculum plan cross the continuity-bias thresholds.

## Overview

This track teaches the **CEO-scope AI agenda at level 70**: enterprise AI strategy, AI portfolio P&L attribution, board-visible AI ROI reporting, external positioning at CEO / board / press / voluntary-code scope, the enterprise AI budget envelope, AI-first workforce transformation (with CHRO), AI investment-case authoring to CFO and board, AI Governance Board / federal CAIO Council leadership under OMB M-25-21, enterprise build-vs-buy at portfolio scope, and cross-functional partnership with CFO / CIO / CTO / CDO / CHRO / GC / CRO / CISO / CMO / CPO. It consumes the `head-of-ai-governance` (level 60) leadership-execution track and the `senior-ai-governance-architect` (level 50) architectural design authority as prerequisite fluency, and adds executive framing — how the CAIRO sits at the executive table, sponsors the enterprise governance function above the head-of-governance role, carries the enterprise AI story to the board and the market, and owns the portfolio economics of the AI budget envelope.

Total planned commitment: **217 hours across 12 modules** + **112 hours across 5 capstone projects** = **~329 hours**.

## Ownership rule

Following the project-wide ownership rule, this curriculum:

- **Owns** the executive AI agenda — enterprise AI strategy at CEO / board scope; AI portfolio P&L attribution and ROI reporting; board / audit-committee / risk-committee reporting cadence; sponsorship of the AI governance function above head-of-ai-governance / Chief Compliance / GC / Chief Privacy / CRO peers; enterprise Responsible AI posture and voluntary-code sign-ons; federal CAIO designation under OMB M-25-21 where applicable; enterprise AI operating model and Center of Excellence design; cross-functional C-suite partnership; enterprise AI talent and workforce transformation; agentic-AI sponsorship at CAIRO scope; enterprise build-vs-buy and AI reference architecture; enterprise AI risk appetite and measurement; enterprise-scope AI incident response leadership; AI security oversight sponsorship; MRM sponsorship in FinServ / Insurance / Pharma; external positioning and industry-body leadership.
- **Defers down** to `head-of-ai-governance-learning` (level 60) for leadership execution of the governance function — running the AI Governance Council, ISO/IEC 42001 AIMS operating calendar, direct regulator engagement, governance-function hiring and P&L; to `senior-ai-governance-architect-learning` (level 50) for architectural design authority; to `ai-evaluation-engineer-learning`, `ai-risk-engineer-learning`, and `ai-governance-analyst-learning` for hands-on assurance, risk engineering, and analyst legwork.
- **Defers sideways** to `agentic-safety-engineer-learning` (level 40) for frontier-agent methodology depth; to `security-learning` and `ai-infra-security-learning` (level 35) for deep ML/AI security at platform scale; and to peer executive tracks in the wider curriculum (`cto-curriculum`, `cpo-curriculum`, `founder-ceo-curriculum`, `startup-operations-governance-curriculum`) for the parts of enterprise strategy the CAIRO coordinates but does not own.
- **Out of scope** to General Counsel and Chief Legal Officer (the CAIRO interfaces; GC delivers the legal opinion); to Chief Privacy Officer / DPO where privacy scope is broader than AI; to Chief Risk Officer where enterprise-risk scope is broader than AI; to Chief Compliance Officer where enterprise-compliance scope is broader than AI; to Chief Audit Executive (independent third line); to Chief Financial Officer (the CAIRO authors the AI investment case; the CFO signs it off).

See [`JOB_REQUIREMENTS.md`](JOB_REQUIREMENTS.md) for the requirements-to-coverage map — 26 in-window postings sampled in the 2026-09-08 live pass and every confirmed requirement theme mapped onto an existing module.

## Module summaries

### mod-101 — AI Governance Foundations
Vocabulary, operating model, and role boundaries every subsequent module assumes. What AI governance is versus AI ethics, AI compliance, and IT governance. The four NIST AI RMF functions placed on a real org chart. IIA Three Lines of Defense applied to AI without collapsing into theatre. The CAIRO role — scope, peers, anti-patterns — grounded in ISO/IEC 38507 and the COSO ERM AI Supplement. Governance operating models (centralised, federated, hub-and-spoke) with the two-strongest-objections test. Failure modes: governance theatre, dual scope, unclear board line.

### mod-102 — Regulatory Landscape
The map of AI rules as they actually exist in 2026. Four lineages (rights-based, sector-based, capability-based, jurisdiction-based). EU AI Act deep read — Articles 6, 9, 11, 43, 72, 73 and Annexes III, IV. NIST AI RMF Playbook sub-functions. Sector-specific regulation (SR 11-7, FRB SR 22-6, NYDFS Part 500, FDA SaMD guidance, EU MDR × AI Act intersection, NAIC Model Law). US state patchwork (CA AI Transparency Act + SB 243, CO Privacy Act AI rules, NY AEDT, TX AI advisory). Jurisdictional mapping as a discipline. Federal OMB M-25-21 CAIO designation shape read from the CAIRO seat.

### mod-103 — AI Risk Frameworks
Turns the framework knowledge from mod-101 and mod-102 into operational discipline. AI risk taxonomy as the unifying spine — NIST AI RMF taxonomy + ISO/IEC 23894 + ERM principles. MAP in practice (NIST MAP-1.1 / MAP-2.x / MAP-5.1 + EU AI Act Art. 9(2)(a)). MEASURE in practice — the leading-vs-lagging trap. MANAGE in practice — residual-risk discipline. GOVERN continuously — closing the loop. The single-system end-to-end capstone linking MAP / MEASURE / MANAGE / GOVERN artifacts.

### mod-104 — Model Risk Management
SR 11-7 as one of the few sources of concrete operational standards for validating, monitoring, and governing a model — applied to AI/ML. Tiering when classical financial-model assumptions do not carry over. Independent validation when challenger models break. SR 11-7-compliant inventory entry for an LLM. The CAIRO × MRM boundary (mod-101 §4 elaborated). MRM outside banking — healthcare (FDA GMLP / PCCP), insurance (NAIC Model Bulletin), public sector, industrial.

### mod-105 — Responsible AI and Ethics
The ethics layer over the risk, regulatory, and MRM discipline. Why AI ethics is a distinct discipline from compliance and risk. The principles landscape (OECD, IEEE 7000, UNESCO, Asilomar, NIST, EU HLEG) without treating the principles as interchangeable. Bias and fairness beyond demographic parity — with the Kleinberg and Chouldechova impossibility results front and centre. Explainability owed to specific audiences, not produced for completeness. Contestability and recourse from the affected-party's perspective. Values into decisions when reasonable people disagree — the ethics-theatre failure mode named and resisted.

### mod-106 — Trust Architecture
The technical and operational machinery by which an organisation decides what an AI agent is permitted to do, on whose behalf, and with what evidence. What "trust" means for AI systems in operational terms. NIST SP 800-207 (Zero Trust) adapted for agentic AI. Identity and capability scoping — W3C Verifiable Credentials, JWT/JOSE, OAuth 2.1, agent passports. Trust scoring — deterministic versus heuristic, with explicit math. Trust gates in the request path. Build-vs-buy-vs-partner decision at enterprise portfolio scope. Agentic-AI sponsorship at CAIRO scope (Citi Agent Factory, NY Life AI Platform shape) lives here as an emerging theme flagged for the next research cycle.

### mod-107 — AI Security and Adversarial Defense
AI security from the CAIRO perspective — program-level fluency, not engineering depth. AI-specific attacks that are live threats in 2026 versus those that remain theoretical. MITRE ATLAS, OWASP LLM Top 10, and NIST AI 100-2 E2023 for threat-modelling without flattening the three into a single checklist. Defense-in-depth for AI systems beyond the classical perimeter. Red-teaming as a governance practice with evidence discipline. The CAIRO × CISO boundary (parallel structure to mod-104 §5). AI-incident classification distinguishing security incidents, AI-program incidents, and joint cases.

### mod-108 — Audit Ledgers and Evidence
Tamper-evident logging, evidence packages, and signed exports — the infrastructure that lets the AI program prove what happened. Operational logging versus audit-grade evidence. Merkle-tree / hash-chain patterns from RFC 9162 without over-loading the cryptographic structure. Event vocabulary at the right granularity — OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions and practitioner patterns. Evidence packages for a specific regulator or auditor request with completeness, signing, and chain of custody. Retention, sealing, and chain-of-custody policies with RFC 3161 timestamping. Build / buy / partner decisions at enterprise portfolio scope.

### mod-109 — Compliance Operations
Turning regulatory obligations into continuous operating practice rather than a quarter-end scramble. Compliance operations versus compliance versus audit versus policy work. Control-mapping discipline from abstract obligation to specific testable control. Continuous-evidence cadence per obligation. What warrants automation versus explicit non-automation. ISO/IEC 42001 Annex A as a working control catalog and its limitations. The CAIRO × Chief Compliance Officer boundary — distinct from CAIRO × MRM and CAIRO × CISO.

### mod-110 — Incident Response
The operational treatment of AI incidents across all classes from mod-107 §6 — with the EU AI Act Article 73 timelines, sector-specific notification obligations (NYDFS Part 500, GDPR Arts 33-34), and the post-incident review discipline that turns incidents into program improvement. The first hour of an AI incident credibly. The notification matrix. Investigation and root-cause analysis that produces improvement rather than blame. Post-incident reviews that survive external scrutiny. Tabletop discipline that tests without over-investing in performance art. Enterprise-face communication to CEO / board / press / customers during a serious incident.

### mod-111 — Board Reporting and Risk Appetite
Where everything below rolls up to executive accountability — the quarterly report, the risk-appetite statement, the materiality framework, the CAIRO at the board table. What boards actually need from CAIRO reporting versus what they don't. AI risk appetite statements that survive board review and operate against actual decisions. Quarterly AI reports the Board Risk Committee can act on. Materiality thresholds. The CAIRO under pressure at the board table. Annual program self-assessment that surfaces meaningful findings. AI portfolio P&L attribution and ROI reporting consolidated for board / audit-committee / CFO consumption.

### mod-112 — The CAIRO Operating Model
Closing module. Ties together the operating model from mod-101, the structural choices made across mod-104 / 106 / 107 / 108 / 109, and the board-facing discipline from mod-111 into a coherent view of what running the function actually looks like over a multi-year horizon. Year-1 operating plan realities. Year-2 consolidation. Year-3 maturity and the stalling warning signs. The CAIRO's calendar and decision rhythm. Talent, budget, and succession without single-load-bearing dependencies. Enterprise AI budget envelope coordinated with the CFO and cross-functional CxO peers.

## Where to go after this curriculum

- **`head-of-ai-governance-learning`** (level 60) — required *prerequisite* rather than a next-step: the CAIRO cannot sponsor the governance function credibly without fluency in the operating discipline the head-of-ai-governance runs.
- **`senior-ai-governance-architect-learning`** (level 50) — deep prerequisite: the CAIRO relies on the architectural design authority of this role and needs enough fluency in it to challenge and defend the design at board scope.
- **`agentic-safety-engineer-learning`** (level 40) — cross-training for frontier-agent methodology depth the CAIRO sponsors and reviews at portfolio scope (Citi Agent Factory, NY Life Enterprise AI Platform shape).
- **`cto-curriculum`** / **`cpo-curriculum`** / **`founder-ceo-curriculum`** — peer executive tracks the CAIRO coordinates with at the C-suite table for reference architecture, product strategy, and enterprise strategy respectively.
