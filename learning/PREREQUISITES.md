# Prerequisites — Chief AI Risk Officer track

The Chief AI Risk Officer curriculum sits at **level 70** on the AI Governance ladder. It is an *executive* track — the reader is preparing to sit at the C-suite table, sponsor the enterprise AI governance function above head-of-ai-governance, own AI portfolio economics, and carry the enterprise AI story to the board, the press, and voluntary-code signatories. What follows is the working knowledge the modules assume you already carry in.

## Lower-level tracks you are expected to have consumed

The CAIRO does not build; the CAIRO sponsors, challenges, defends. But the sponsorship is defensible only if the reader has depth in what the layer immediately below runs.

- **`head-of-ai-governance-learning`** (level 60) — required. Everything about running the governance function day-to-day — the AI Governance Council operating rhythm, the ISO/IEC 42001 AIMS operating calendar, direct regulator engagement (EU AI Office + national CAs, US AISI + AISIC, sector regulators), enterprise regulatory-change management, voluntary-code sign-on decision-making, program measurement and maturity assessment, function hiring and P&L. The CAIRO track *sponsors* this function; it does not repeat its curriculum. If any of that reads unfamiliar, work through the head-of-ai-governance packet before starting mod-101 here.
- **`senior-ai-governance-architect-learning`** (level 50) — deep prerequisite. The control library, policy taxonomy, cross-jurisdiction crosswalk, ISO/IEC 42001 AIMS scope, risk taxonomy and appetite architecture, assurance and evidence architecture, third-party program, post-market surveillance architecture, GRC-for-AI reference architecture, operating model, sector blueprints. The CAIRO consumes this architecture; the CAIRO must be fluent enough to challenge and defend it at board scope.
- **`ai-evaluation-engineer-learning`** (level 35) — recommended. The release-assurance / audit-trail / regulator-facing evaluation methodology the CAIRO signs off on.
- **`ai-risk-engineer-learning`** (level 25) — recommended. The hands-on engineering craft of AI risk the CAIRO reviews at portfolio scope.
- **`ai-governance-analyst-learning`** (level 15) — recommended. The operational analyst legwork the CAIRO staffs and directs.

## Working knowledge assumed

You do not need to have shipped any of these yourself, but you should be able to read them without stopping every few paragraphs to look up vocabulary.

- **NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0** — the four functions (GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE), the Playbook depth, the GOVERN function as the leadership shape.
- **ISO/IEC 42001** — the AIMS operating calendar, Annex A control catalog, and where 42006 audit-body inspection quality sits against a certifiable program.
- **ISO/IEC 38507** — the definitional shape for board-facing responsibilities in AI governance.
- **IIA Three Lines Model at leadership scope** — first line, second line, third line, and how the CAIRO sponsors the second line without operational-distance failure.
- **COSO ERM AI Supplement** and **NACD AI Oversight Blueprint** — board-facing risk-oversight reference shapes.
- **EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)** — the risk-tier taxonomy, Article 9 RMS, Article 11 technical documentation, Article 12 record-keeping, Article 13 transparency, Article 14 human oversight, Article 43 conformity assessment, Article 72 post-market monitoring, Article 73 serious-incident reporting, Chapter VII (EU AI Office, EU AI Board, national CAs), Annexes III and IV. Where relevant to the reader's sector, the GPAI Code of Practice work.
- **SR 11-7** — the OCC/FRB Model Risk Management guidance in full (about 21 pages), plus the ML/AI extensions (FRB SR 22-6, EBA Model Risk Mgmt GL).
- **OMB M-25-21** and **OMB M-25-22** and **EO 14179** — where the reader is operating in a federal-agency context, the CAIO designation, the AI Governance Board equivalence, the federal CAIO Council posture.
- **Frontier-lab tiered risk frameworks** — Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy, OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, Google DeepMind's Frontier Safety Framework — read as shape references for enterprise risk-tier language.
- **MITRE ATLAS + OWASP LLM Top 10 + NIST AI 100-2 E2023** — read as taxonomies, not checklists.
- **Cross-industry public benchmarks** — PwC 2026 CAIRO priorities, IBM 2026 CEO Study, KPMG board-of-directors AI oversight surveys.

## Executive posture assumed

- **You have already led a function.** The CAIRO track does not teach how to lead a team; it teaches how to lead a specific enterprise function. Any of the following is a plausible entry shape: senior-VP-scope enterprise-technology role (CIO / CTO / CDO), functional-VP-scope Risk / Compliance / Audit / Privacy / Legal role with an AI-heavy remit, a head-of-ai-governance seat, a public-sector SES-equivalent with a technology portfolio, or a frontier-lab program-leadership seat.
- **You have carried a P&L.** Any of the CAIRO postings in [`JOB_REQUIREMENTS.md`](JOB_REQUIREMENTS.md) require ownership of a budget line at scale. If you have not previously carried a budget with material variance, work through `startup-finance-fundraising-curriculum` or a peer finance-fundamentals track first.
- **You have sat at a board or audit committee.** Or you have been the author of the materials that were reviewed by one. The CAIRO track's board-reporting module (mod-111) assumes familiarity with the room; it does not teach how the room works.
- **You are comfortable in front of regulators.** The CAIRO does not run regulator engagement day-to-day (that owns down to head-of-ai-governance at level 60) but shows up in serious matters. If regulator-facing communication is a new experience, back-fill through the head-of-ai-governance mod-104 (direct regulator engagement) before proceeding here.

## Not taught here — you carry it in

These are peer-CxO scopes the CAIRO coordinates with but does not own. If a topic is load-bearing for your intended CAIRO shape and you do not currently have the depth, back-fill from the peer track before starting.

- **Legal opinion delivery** — GC / CLO scope. The CAIRO briefs and challenges the GC; the GC delivers the legal opinion. Nothing in this track teaches how to author a legal opinion.
- **Enterprise privacy program broader than AI** — CPO / DPO scope.
- **Enterprise risk broader than AI** — CRO scope. See `startup-operations-governance-curriculum` for enterprise-risk fundamentals, or an ERM-specific reading list.
- **Deep ML / AI security engineering** — `security-learning` and `ai-infra-security-learning` at level 35. The CAIRO sponsors and reviews the AI-security program at portfolio scope; the CAIRO does not build defences.
- **Hands-on ML / AI engineering** — `ml-engineer-learning`, `ai-infra-engineer-learning`, `agentic-ai-engineer-learning`. The CAIRO understands and sponsors; the CAIRO does not implement.
- **Enterprise product strategy at CPO scope** — `cpo-curriculum`. The CAIRO coordinates on AI-product portfolio; the CPO owns product P&L.
- **Enterprise technology strategy at CTO scope** — `cto-curriculum`. The CAIRO partners on AI reference architecture; the CTO owns technology strategy.
- **Founder / CEO scope** — `founder-ceo-curriculum`. The CAIRO reports into or matrixes with the CEO; the CEO owns the enterprise agenda.

## Recommended reading before mod-101

If you have the option, work through the following in the order shown before the first module. None is required — the modules are self-contained — but each anchors the executive-scope reading you are about to do.

1. **NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0** and the **Playbook**, cover to cover. About 4–6 hours.
2. **ISO/IEC 38507** — Governance implications of the use of artificial intelligence by organizations. About 2 hours.
3. **EU AI Act** — the recitals, Chapter II (prohibited AI), Chapter III (high-risk AI systems, particularly Articles 9, 11, 12, 13, 14, 43, 72, 73), Chapter VII (governance). Approximately a working day.
4. **PwC 2026 CAIRO priorities** and the **IBM 2026 CEO Study** AI sections. Approximately 3–4 hours for both.
5. **Anthropic Responsible Scaling Policy** (current version), **OpenAI Preparedness Framework** (current version), and **Google DeepMind Frontier Safety Framework** (current version). About 3 hours for all three.
6. **NACD Director's Handbook on AI Oversight** (or the current equivalent). About 3 hours.
7. **IIA Three Lines Model** (2020 revision) and **COSO ERM AI Supplement**. About 2 hours together.
8. **OMB M-25-21**, if your intended CAIRO shape is federal or federal-adjacent. About 1 hour.

That is roughly 20 hours of prior reading. Spend the time — the modules assume it.
